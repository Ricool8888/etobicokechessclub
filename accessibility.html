Accessibility

How this website tries to work for everyone, and how to tell us when it does not.

Last reviewed: November 10, 2026

Our commitment

The Etobicoke Chess Club wants everyone to be able to read this site and find what they need, whether they use a keyboard, a screen reader, a phone, or a browser with large text.

1. The standard we aim for

We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, Level AA — the standard Ontario’s accessibility rules are built on.

2. What the site does

  • Keyboard. Every page can be used without a mouse, including the menus, the calendar, the tournament tabs, the FAQ answers, the photo gallery and its larger viewer, the share menu and the theme switch. Focus is always visible. In the month calendar, the arrow keys move between days, Home and End jump to the ends of the week, Enter opens a day, and Escape closes it and puts focus back on the day you opened.
  • Skip link. The first thing you can tab to on any page is “Skip to main content”, which jumps past the menu.
  • Contrast and colour. Text is written to meet the 4.5:1 contrast minimum in both the dark and the light theme, and nothing is explained by colour alone: the calendar uses a circle, a square, a diamond and an X for meetings, events and club closures, with the same shapes repeated in the legend.
  • Zoom and small screens. Pages are built to reflow on narrow screens and to stay usable when text is enlarged.
  • Motion. Animations and smooth scrolling stop when your device asks for reduced motion.
  • Screen readers. Pages declare their language, the current page is marked in the menu, the calendar’s month is announced when it changes, day cells describe what is on them, and photos carry descriptions.
  • Third-party content is opt-in. The live Lichess board, the meeting map and the engine evaluation only load when you ask for them, so a page can be read without contacting those services.

3. Known limitations

  • Maps are drawn by Google and only load when you press Show map. The full street address is always printed as text above the map, so you never need the map.
  • Tournament results live on chess-results.com, which is another organisation’s page and outside our control. If you need a result read out to you, email us and we will help.
  • The live game board, the puzzle board and the evaluation bar are third-party widgets (Lichess, chessground and a Stockfish engine). They are never the only way to get the information on the page.
  • Some photos have short descriptions because the visible caption already says what is happening. If you need a photo described in more detail, ask and we will help.

4. Tell us about a barrier

If something on this site is hard or impossible for you to use, email etobicokechessclub@gmail.com and describe the page and what happened. Please include the address of the page and, if you can, the browser or assistive technology you were using.

We will reply as soon as we reasonably can. If we cannot fix the problem quickly, we will send you the information in another format — by email, in large print, or over the phone — so you are not blocked in the meantime.

5. Keeping this page current

We review accessibility whenever the site changes, and we check every page with a keyboard before a release. This page is updated when that review finds something worth reporting.
